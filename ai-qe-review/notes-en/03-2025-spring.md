## A. Machine Learning Theory

**Question: A1: Variance of empirical loss** Fixed binary classification hypothesis $h$, prove that when the sample size is $m$, the variance of empirical loss $L_S(h)$ is $L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h)(1-L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h))/m$. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 1 **Official English prompt.** Let $\mathcal H$ be a class of binary classifiers over $X$, $D$ an unknown distribution, $f\in\mathcal H$ the target, and fix $h\in\mathcal H$. Show that the variance, over samples $S$ of size $m$ drawn from $D$, of the empirical loss $L_S(h)$ is inversely proportional to $m$. More specifically, prove $\operatorname{Var}_{S\mid x\sim D^m}[L_S(h)]=L_{D,f}(h)(1-L_{D,f}(h))/m$. **English interpretation.** Let $\mathcal H$ be the binary classifier class, $D$ be the unknown distribution, $f$ be the target hypothesis, fixed $h$. Prove that the variance of the empirical loss $L_S(h)$ on an independent sample of size $m$ is inversely proportional to $m$, and give the exact formula $L_{D,f}(h)(1-L_{D,f}(h))/m$. 

**Answer. ** Let 

$$
Z_i=\mathbf{1}[h(x_i)\ne f(x_i)],
  \qquad
  p=L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h).
$$

 due to $x_i\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{D}$, therefore $Z_i\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bern}(p)$. The experience loss is 

$$
L_S(h)=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_i.
$$

Independence gives 

$$
\operatorname{Var}_S(L_S(h))
  =
  \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_i\right)
  =
  \frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)
  =
  \frac{p(1-p)}{m}.
$$

Replace $p$ $L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h)$, we get 

$$
\operatorname{Var}_S(L_S(h))
  =
  \frac{L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h)\bigl(1-L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h)\bigr)}{m}.
$$

. This is the mean variance reduction of $O(1/m)$.

** Question: A3: VC dimension of the union** Suppose the VC dimensions of $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{H}'$ are finite. Prove that the 

$$
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{H}')
  \le
  \operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H})
  +
  \operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}')
  +1.
$$

 union is used for the union of the two-dimensional axis-parallel rectangle class and the triangle class. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 2 **Official English prompt.** Let $\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal H'$ be two families from $X$ to $\{0,1\}$ with finite VC dimensions. (a) Show $\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H\cup\mathcal H')\le\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H)+\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H')+1$. (b) Use this to determine the VC dimension of the union of axis-aligned rectangles and triangles in dimension 2; you may use that the triangle class has VC dimension 7. **English interpretation.** Let $\mathcal H,\mathcal H'$ be a finite VC-dimensional function family from $X$ to $\{0,1\}$. (a) Prove the upper bound of the VC dimension of the union class; (b) Apply the conclusion to the union of the two-dimensional axis-parallel rectangle and the triangle class. The VC dimension of the triangle class can be directly taken to be 7. 

**Answer. ** Let $d=\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H})$, $d'=\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}')$, $m=d+d'+2$. If $\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{H}'$ can shatter a certain $m$ point set, then all $2^m$ marks on the point set can be shattered by $\mathcal{H}$ or $\mathcal{H}'$ Realized, therefore 

$$
2^m
  \le
  \tau_{\mathcal{H}}(m)+\tau_{\mathcal{H}'}(m).
$$

 by [[02-primer#VC dimension, growth function and Sauer's lemma|Sauer's lemma]], 

$$
\tau_{\mathcal{H}}(m)
  \le
  \sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{m}{i},
  \qquad
  \tau_{\mathcal{H}'}(m)
  \le
  \sum_{i=0}^{d'}\binom{m}{i}.
$$

 because $d+d'=m-2$, both tails leave at least one term on the complementary side. Using the symmetry of the binomial coefficients we get 

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{m}{i}
  +
  \sum_{i=0}^{d'}\binom{m}{i}
  <
  2^m.
$$

 which contradicts the shatter hypothesis. Therefore, $m=d+d'+2$ points cannot be shattered, so the VC dimension of the 

$$
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{H}')
  \le d+d'+1.
$$

 two-dimensional axis-parallel rectangle is $4$. The question allows the direct use of the VC dimension of the two-dimensional triangle class to be $7$. So 

$$
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rect}}\cup\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{tri}})
  \le 4+7+1=12.
$$

**Exam reminder. **Strictly speaking, the above conclusion gives the upper bound of union. If the question understands "determine" as "use the theorem just proved to give the bound required for the exam", the answer is $12$ the upper bound; if exact VC dimension is required, an additional lower bound needs to be constructed, and the original question does not provide enough hints. 

**Question: A4: Stability and regularized ERM** Prove that on-average replace-one stability derives the expected generalization bound; then prove the risk bound of regularized ERM under convex Lipschitz loss. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 2 **Official English prompt.** (a) Let $S=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)$ be i.i.d. from $D$. If learning algorithm $A$ is on-average replace-one stable at rate $\varepsilon(m)$, prove $\mathbb E_{S\sim D^m}[L_D(A(S))-L_S(A(S))]\le\varepsilon(m)$. (b) Let $\ell$ be convex and $\rho$-Lipschitz. Prove regularized ERM satisfies $\mathbb E_S L_D(A(S))\le L_D(w^*)+\lambda\|w^*\|^2+2\rho^2/(\lambda m)$, where $w^*=\arg\min_{w\in\mathcal H}L_D(w)$. **English interpretation.** (a) If the algorithm has on-average replace-one stability rate $\varepsilon(m)$, prove that its expected generalization difference does not exceed this amount; (b) For convex and $\rho$-Lipschitz losses, prove that the expected risk of regularized ERM satisfies the problem given $\lambda\|w^*\|^2+2\rho^2/(\lambda m)$ Upper bound. 

**Answer. ** (a) Assume $S=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)$, let $z_i'$ be an independent and identically distributed replacement sample, and $S^{(i)}$ be the data set in which $z_i$ is replaced by $z_i'$. By independence and symmetry, 

$$
\mathbb{E}_S L_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S))
  =
  \frac1m\sum_{i=1}^{m}
  \mathbb{E}_{S,z_i'} \ell(A(S),z_i'),
$$

 and 

$$
\mathbb{E}_S L_S(A(S))
  =
  \frac1m\sum_{i=1}^{m}
  \mathbb{E}_S \ell(A(S),z_i).
$$

 due to $S^{(i)}$ and $S$ Identical distribution, 

$$
\mathbb{E}_S \ell(A(S),z_i)
  =
  \mathbb{E}_{S,z_i'} \ell(A(S^{(i)}),z_i').
$$

 subtract the two equations to get 

$$
\mathbb{E}_S\!\left[
    L_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S))-L_S(A(S))
  \right]
  =
  \frac1m\sum_{i=1}^{m}
  \mathbb{E}
  \left[
    \ell(A(S),z_i')-\ell(A(S^{(i)}),z_i')
  \right]
  \le \varepsilon(m).
$$

$b$ For regularized ERM, $\lambda\|w\|^2$ in the objective function provides $2\lambda$-Strong convexity. For the $\rho$-Lipschitz convex loss, the standard stability derivation gives the magnitude 

$$
\varepsilon(m)\le \frac{2\rho^2}{\lambda m}.
$$

 and because $A(S)$ To minimize the empirical risk of regularization, 

$$
L_S(A(S))+\lambda\|A(S)\|^2
  \le
  L_S(w^*)+\lambda\|w^*\|^2.
$$

 takes the expectation and discards the non-negative terms $\lambda\|A(S)\|^2$, with 

$$
\mathbb{E}_S L_S(A(S))
  \le
  L_{\mathcal{D}}(w^*)+\lambda\|w^*\|^2.
$$

 plus the stability and generalization gap, we get 

$$
\mathbb{E}_S L_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S))
  \le
  L_{\mathcal{D}}(w^*)+\lambda\|w^*\|^2+\frac{2\rho^2}{\lambda m}.
$$

**Answer. ** Let the training set be $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $x_i\in\mathbb{R}^d$, $y_i\in\{1,\ldots,C\}$. Define feature extractor

$$
\phi_{\theta_f}:\mathbb{R}^d\to \mathbb{R}^r
$$

 and classifier

$$
c_{\theta_c}:\mathbb{R}^r\to \mathbb{R}^C.
$$

 network output logits

$$
a_\theta(x)=c_{\theta_c}(\phi_{\theta_f}(x)),
  \qquad
  p_\theta(y=c|x)=
  \frac{\exp(a_{\theta,c}(x))}{\sum_{j=1}^{C}\exp(a_{\theta,j}(x))}.
$$

 Suppose the space is 

$$
\mathcal{H}=
  \{x\mapsto \operatorname{softmax}(c_{\theta_c}(\phi_{\theta_f}(x))):\theta=(\theta_f,\theta_c)\}.
$$

The cross entropy loss is 

$$
\widehat L(\theta)
  =
  -\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \log p_\theta(y_i|x_i).
$$

SGD Approximate gradient on minibatch $B_k$: 

$$
\theta_{k+1}
  =
  \theta_k-\eta_k
  \frac{1}{|B_k|}
  \sum_{i\in B_k}
  \nabla_\theta[-\log p_\theta(y_i|x_i)].

Batch normalization pre-activation of a batch $u$ When doing 

$$
\hat u=\frac{u-\mu_B}{\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\epsilon}},

  \operatorname{BN}(u)=\gamma\hat u+\beta.

, use batch statistics and update running mean/variance when training; use running statistics when testing to avoid random output caused by a single sample. 

If the training error is high, it indicates insufficient expression or optimization. If the training error is low and the test error is high, it indicates overfitting. **Question: B2: VP SDE and score matching** Given VP SDE

$$
dx=-\frac12\beta(t)x\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dW_t,
$$

 write reverse-time SDE, prove the denoising score matching identity, and give the conditional distribution and training loss. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 3 **Official English prompt.** For the variance-preserving SDE $dx=-\tfrac 12\beta(t)x\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dW_t$: (a) write the reverse-time SDE and explain the score-matching objective $\min_\theta\mathbb E_t\lambda(t)\mathbb E_{x_t}\|s_\theta(x_t,t)-\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t)\|^2$; (b) prove its denoising-score-matching conditional form differs only by a constant; (c) find $p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)$ and derive the final denoising loss. **English interpretation.** For VP SDE: (a) Write reverse time SDE and explain why score matching is needed; (b) Prove that the edge score target and the conditional denoising score matching target only differ by a constant; (c) Find $p_{t|0}(x_t\mid x_0)$ and derive the final denoising score-matching loss. 

**Answer. ** Generally, the reverse-time SDE of forward SDE is 

$$
dx=
  \left[
    f(x,t)-g(t)^2\nabla_x\log p_t(x)
  \right]dt
  +
  g(t)\,d\bar W_t,
$$

 where $dt$ along the reverse time is negative. For VP SDE, $f(x,t)=-\beta(t)x/2$, $g(t)=\sqrt{\beta(t)}$, so 

$$
dx=
  \left[
    -\frac12\beta(t)x-\beta(t)\nabla_x\log p_t(x)
  \right]dt
  +
  \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\bar W_t.
$$

 therefore needs to train the network $s_\theta(x_t,t)\approx \nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t)$, that is, check back the training signal of [[02-primer#Diffusion and score|diffusion score]]. 

The following proves denoising identity. For any function $s_\theta$, expand the square: 

$$
\mathbb{E}_{x_t}\|s_\theta(x_t,t)-\nabla\log p_t(x_t)\|^2.
$$

Use 

$$
p_t(x_t)=\int p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)p_{\text{data}}(x_0)\,dx_0
$$

 and Fisher identity, 

$$
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t)
  =
  \mathbb{E}[\nabla_{x_t}\log p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)\mid x_t].
$$

 Let $Y=\nabla\log p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)$, then $\mathbb{E}[Y|x_t]=\nabla\log p_t(x_t)$. Conditional variance decomposition gives 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|s_\theta-\mathbb{E}[Y|x_t]\|^2
  =
  \mathbb{E}\|s_\theta-Y\|^2
  -
  \mathbb{E}\|Y-\mathbb{E}[Y|x_t]\|^2.
$$

The second term is independent of $\theta$, so 

$$
\mathbb{E}_{x_t}\|s_\theta-\nabla\log p_t\|^2
  =
  \mathbb{E}_{x_0,x_t}\|s_\theta-\nabla\log p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)\|^2+C.
$$

For VP SDE, solve for linear SDE We get 

$$
x_t=\alpha(t)x_0+\sqrt{1-\alpha(t)^2}\,\varepsilon,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon\sim \mathcal{N}(0,I),
$$

 where 

$$
\alpha(t)=\exp\left(-\frac12\int_0^t\beta(s)\,ds\right).
$$

 so 

$$
p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)
  =
  \mathcal{N}\left(\alpha(t)x_0,\bigl(1-\alpha(t)^2\bigr)I\right),
$$

 and 

$$
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_{t|0}(x_t|x_0)
  =
  -\frac{x_t-\alpha(t)x_0}{1-\alpha(t)^2}.
$$

The final training goal can be written as 

$$
\min_\theta
  \mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\varepsilon}
  \lambda(t)
  \left\|
    s_\theta\!\left(\alpha(t)x_0+\sqrt{1-\alpha(t)^2}\varepsilon,t\right)
    +
    \frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1-\alpha(t)^2}}
  \right\|^2.
$$

**Question: C1-C6: prox-SGD convergence recursion** Consider $f$ as $L$-smooth, $\mu$-strongly convex, stochastic gradient $g(x,\xi)=\nabla f(x)+\xi$, $\mathbb{E}\xi=0$, $\mathbb{E}\|\xi\|^2\le \sigma^2$, and use 

$$
x_{k+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma R}(x_k-\gamma g(x_k,\xi^k))
$$

 to solve $\min_x f(x)+R(x)$. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 3--4 **Official English prompt.** Let $f$ be $L$-smooth and $\mu$-strongly convex, $g(x,\xi)=\nabla f(x)+\xi$ with zero-mean noise and second moment at most $\sigma^2$, and $x_{k+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma R}(x_k-\gamma g(x_k,\xi_k))$. (1) define $L$-smoothness and $\mu$-strong convexity; (2) combine them via $g(x)=f(x)-\mu\|x\|^2/2$ to prove the stated co-coercivity inequality; (3) derive the prox nonexpansive recurrence; (4) apply variance decomposition; (5) at $\gamma=2/(\mu+L)$, obtain the simplified contraction; (6) prove the stated precision/iteration complexity bound and noise floor. **English interpretation.** Six questions about the stochastic gradient method containing prox: define $L$-smooth and $\mu$-strong convex; combine the two to derive a co-coercivity type inequality; use prox nonexpansiveness to recurse; perform variance decomposition; in $\gamma=2/(\mu+L)$ Compression is obtained; finally, the iteration complexity and noise floor of the specified accuracy are proved. 

**Answer. ** **Definition. ** [[02-primer#Smoothness, strong convexity and the descent lemma|$L$-smooth and $\mu$-strong convex]] is given in chapter [[02-primer|Theoretical Foundations]]. Put 

$$
g_0(x)=f(x)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x\|^2
$$

 then $g_0$ convex, and $(L-\mu)$-smooth. Its gradient is 

$$
\nabla g_0(x)=\nabla f(x)-\mu x.
$$

Use cocoercivity for the convex smooth function: 

$$
\frac{1}{L-\mu}
  \|\nabla g_0(x)-\nabla g_0(y)\|^2
  \le
  \langle \nabla g_0(x)-\nabla g_0(y),x-y\rangle.
$$

Expand and organize, you can get the inequality form required in the question: 

$$
\mu\|x-y\|^2+
  \frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^2
  \le
  \left(1+\frac{\mu}{L}\right)
  \langle \nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle.
$$

**prox recursion. ** [[02-primer#Proximal Optimality, Monotonicity, and Nonexpansiveness|prox optimality condition]] shows that the optimal point $x^*$ satisfies 

$$
x^*=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma R}(x^*-\gamma\nabla f(x^*)).
$$

 by [[02-primer#Proximal Optimality, Monotonicity, and Nonexpansiveness|prox nonexpansiveness]], 

$$
\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_k-\gamma(\nabla f(x_k)+\xi^k)-x^*+\gamma\nabla f(x^*)\|^2.
$$

 is 

$$
\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_k-x^*-\gamma(\nabla f(x_k)-\nabla f(x^*)+\xi^k)\|^2.
$$

** variance decomposition. ** Conditioned on $x_k$, let 

$$
a=x_k-x^*-\gamma(\nabla f(x_k)-\nabla f(x^*)).
$$

 then 

$$
\mathbb{E}_k\|a-\gamma\xi^k\|^2
  =
  \|a\|^2
  -2\gamma\langle a,\mathbb{E}_k\xi^k\rangle
  +
  \gamma^2\mathbb{E}_k\|\xi^k\|^2.
$$

 due to $\mathbb{E}_k\xi^k=0$, 

$$
\mathbb{E}_k\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_k-x^*-\gamma(\nabla f(x_k)-\nabla f(x^*))\|^2
  +
  \gamma^2\sigma^2.
$$

** linear shrinkage. ** Take $\gamma=2/(L+\mu)$. Expand the deterministic term: 

$$
\|u-\gamma v\|^2
  =
  \|u\|^2-2\gamma\langle u,v\rangle+\gamma^2\|v\|^2,
$$

 among which $u=x_k-x^*$, $v=\nabla f(x_k)-\nabla f(x^*)$. Using the strong convex-smooth joint inequality above, we get 

$$
\|u-\gamma v\|^2
  \le
  \left(1-\frac{4\mu L}{(L+\mu)^2}\right)\|u\|^2.
$$

 note

$$
\rho=\frac{4\mu L}{(L+\mu)^2}.
$$

 by tower property, 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  (1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\|x_k-x^*\|^2+\gamma^2\sigma^2.
$$

 recursively expands to 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|x_k-x^*\|^2
  \le
  (1-\rho)^k\|x_0-x^*\|^2
  +
  \frac{\gamma^2\sigma^2}{\rho}.
$$

 is also available for small steps 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|x_k-x^*\|^2
  \le
  (1-\gamma\mu)^k\|x_0-x^*\|^2+\frac{\gamma\sigma^2}{\mu}.
$$

If you want the first term not to exceed $\varepsilon\|x_0-x^*\|^2$, just 

$$
k\ge \frac{1}{\gamma\mu}\log\frac1\varepsilon.
$$

 select $\gamma$ with the condition number $\kappa=L/\mu$ Stable step sizes of the same order can be obtained in the form of 

$$
k\ge \frac{L/\mu+3}{4}\log\frac1\varepsilon,
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}\|x_k-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \varepsilon\|x_0-x^*\|^2+\frac{\gamma\sigma^2}{\mu}.
$$

. The last term is the noise floor of stochastic gradient; it cannot disappear unless $\gamma\to0$ or the variance is reduced.

**Questions: D1-D5: Concept questions** Answers trigram smoothing, representation learning, top-$p$, LLM success factors and the advantages of Transformer over RNN. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 5 **Official English prompt.** Short Answer Questions on Concepts. **Answer. ** **Trigram. ** The maximum likelihood estimate is 

$$
\hat p(w_3|w_1,w_2)=
  \frac{c(w_1,w_2,w_3)}{c(w_1,w_2)}.
$$

If the context does not appear, smoothing/backoff/interpolation is required, such as 

$$
p(w_3|w_1,w_2)
  =
  \lambda_3\hat p_{\mathrm{tri}}
  +
  \lambda_2\hat p_{\mathrm{bi}}
  +
  \lambda_1\hat p_{\mathrm{uni}}.
$$

Kneser-Ney smoothing can also be used. 

**Representation learning. ** The central idea is to learn low-dimensional continuous representations from data relationships, making semantic, structural or task-related similarities computable in vector space. Typical algorithms include autoencoder, word2vec, GloVe, contrastive learning, BERT/Transformer encoder, and graph embedding. 

**Top-$p$ sampling. ** Greedy/beam search is prone to generating high-probability but repetitive and degraded text. Top-$p$ samples from the minimum candidate set with a cumulative probability of $p$, and the number of candidates adapts with the distribution entropy; compared with top-$k$, it will not forcibly retain too many low-quality words in sharp distributions, nor will it prematurely truncate reasonable words in flat distributions. 

**LLM Success Factors. ** Key include large-scale [[02-primer#LLM pre-training, instruction fine-tuning and alignment|Self-supervised pre-training]], [[02-primer#Transformer self-attention|Transformer can be optimized in parallel]], data and parameters [[02-primer#Scaling Law|scaling law]], instruction tuning, [[02-primer#LLM preference alignment|RLHF/DPO alignment]], retrieval/tool usage, high-throughput training and inference optimization in systems engineering. 

**Transformer Advantages. ** Compared with LSTM/GRU, [[02-primer#Transformer self-attention|self-attention]] has a shorter path length and more direct long-range dependencies; training can be parallelized and hardware utilization is high; multi-head attention can capture different relationships in different subspaces; residuals and normalization make deep training more stable. 

** Question: D6: Explain data scaling law from the perspective of statistical learning** Explain why increasing sample size will bring regular performance improvements, and the data cannot be assumed to be simple Gaussian. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 5 **Official English prompt.** Explain why the data scaling law relating performance to sample size holds from the perspective of statistical learning. Give a detailed analysis and avoid overly simplistic assumptions such as Gaussian data. **English interpretation.** Explain the data scaling law of the relationship between performance and sample size from the perspective of statistical learning, give a detailed analysis, and do not rely on oversimplified assumptions such as "the data is Gaussian". 

**Answer. ** Assuming that the model class and training algorithm are fixed, the generalization error can be roughly divided into approximation, estimation and optimization: 

$$
L(\hat h_n)-L^*
  =
  \underbrace{L(h_{\mathcal{H}}^*)-L^*}_{\text{approximation}}
  +
  \underbrace{L(\hat h_n)-L(h_{\mathcal{H}}^*)}_{\text{estimation}}
  +
  \underbrace{\text{optimization gap}}_{\text{training}}.
$$

 Sample size $n$ Increase mainly reduces estimation error. For the limited VC/Rademacher complexity class, uniform convergence gives $O(n^{-1/2})$ or a finer power law; for over-parametric neural networks, empirically the superposition of different error sources often results in 

$$
L(n)=L_\infty+A n^{-\alpha}.
$$

. This does not require data Gaussian. It relies on sample independence or weak dependence, stable task distribution, and the ability of models and optimization to transform new sample information into effective constraints. The scaling law will deviate if the data is duplicated, contaminated, has a shifted distribution, or lacks model capacity. 

**Question: D7: RLHF Policy Optimization** Assuming that the reward model has been trained, explain how to use reinforcement learning to align LLM with human preferences, including data, loss, and gradient. **Source positioning:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 5 **Official English prompt.** A reward model for RLHF training of an LLM has already been trained. Explain how reinforcement learning aligns the model to human preferences, including data usage, the RL loss function, and its gradient. **English interpretation.** The reward model has been trained. Showing how to align an LLM with human preferences through reinforcement learning must include how the data is used, the reinforcement learning loss function, and the gradient of that loss. 

**Answer. ** This question writes [[02-primer#LLM preference alignment|RLHF]] as a sequence decision-making problem, and reviews the policy gradient skeleton [[02-primer#Policy gradient and baseline|Chapter 3 Derivation of policy gradient]]. 

Given prompt $x$, strategy $\pi_\theta(y|x)$ generates the answer $y$. The reward model gives $r_\psi(x,y)$. The commonly used goal is the expected reward with KL regularity: 

$$
J(\theta)
  =
  \mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim \pi_\theta}
  \left[
    r_\psi(x,y)
    -
    \beta
    \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y|x)}
  \right].
$$

 Treat the entire answer as an action trajectory, and the policy gradient is 

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
  =
  \mathbb{E}
  \left[
    \nabla_\theta\log \pi_\theta(y|x)
    \left(
      r_\psi(x,y)
      -
      \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y|x)}
      -
      b(x)
    \right)
  \right],
$$

where $b(x)$ is Baseline/value model, used to reduce variance. The PPO version will add clipped ratio to control the policy update so that it does not deviate too far from the old policy. 

**Question: D8: RAG system design without annotated domain QA** There are domain documents but no Q&A annotations. Design a system that can answer domain questions. **Source positioning:** Official examination: 2025 Spring, p. 5--6 **Official English prompt.** A general-purpose LLM performs poorly at QA in a specific domain because it lacks domain knowledge. A large corpus of domain documents is available, but there are no labeled QA pairs. Design a system that accurately answers questions in this domain; you may add components such as embedding models. **English interpretation.** General LLM performs very poorly in QA in a specialized field due to lack of domain knowledge. There are a large number of domain documents but no question-answer pairs are annotated. Design a system that can accurately answer questions in this field, and you can add components such as embedding. 

**Answer. ** The minimum reliable solution is [[02-primer#Embedding knowledge base reasoning, RAG and MLN|RAG]]: 

1. **Document processing:** Cleaning, chunking, retaining metadata such as title/chapter/page number/time. 

2. **Index:** Use the domain embedding model to generate chunk vectors and establish vector indexes; while retaining the BM25 keyword index. 

3. **Search:** Do query rewriting for the question $q$, mix dense retrieval and sparse retrieval, and take top-$k$. 

4. **Reranking:** Use cross-encoder/reranker to filter irrelevant chunks. 

5. **Generate:** Enter evidence fragments, citation information, and constraint prompts into LLM, requiring answers based solely on evidence. 

6. **Check:** After outputting the answer, do citation check, answerability check and hallucination detector. 

When there is no QA annotation, you can use the document to automatically generate synthetic QA, and then manually sample and verify it; you can also use contrastive or instruction-tuning to fine-tune the embedding/reranker. Evaluation metrics include retrieval recall@$k$, faithfulness, answer exactness, and human expert ratings.
