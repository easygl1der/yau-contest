**Question: A13: sigmoid network approximation of Lipschitz function** For any $\rho$-Lipschitz’s $f:[-1,1]^n\to[-1,1]$ and $\varepsilon>0$, construct the sigmoid network $N$ using $|f(x)-N(x)|\le\varepsilon$. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Fall, p. 3 **Official English prompt.** Neural networks are universal approximators: let $f:[-1,1]^n\to[-1,1]$ be $\rho$-Lipschitz and fix $\varepsilon>0$. Construct a sigmoid network $N:[-1,1]^n\to[-1,1]$ with $|f(x)-N(x)|\le\varepsilon$ for every $x$. The prompt is to divide the small square box, approximate the constant in the box, and then use the network to approximate the identification and value of the box. 

**Answer. ** Divide $[-1,1]^n$ into small squares with side length 

$$
\Delta\le \frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho\sqrt n}
$$

. For each small square $Q_j$, replace the representative point $c_j$ and let $v_j=f(c_j)$. If $x\in Q_j$, Lipschitz property gives 

$$
|f(x)-v_j|\le \rho\|x-c_j\|\le \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$

 Then use sigmoid unit to approximate the indicator of each coordinate interval. Because

$$
\sigma(\alpha(x-a))-\sigma(\alpha(x-b))
$$

Approximately $\mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}(x)$ when $\alpha$ is large enough, the multidimensional block indicator can be implemented by the approximate product of the coordinate indicators; the product can in turn be approximated by a small feedforward network. Finally, let 

$$
N(x)=\sum_j v_j\,\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{Q_j}(x).
$$

 When the approximation error of each indicator is small enough, the total error is the sum of the block constant error and the indicator approximation error, controlled within $\varepsilon$. This gives a constructive universal approximation.

The second term is 

$$
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^2
  \le 2L D_f(x,y).
$$

 merge to get 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|g(x)-g(y)\|^2
  \le
  2
  \left[
    \frac1\tau \max_i\frac{L_i}{nq_i}
    +
    \left(1-\frac1\tau\right)L
  \right]
  D_f(x,y).
$$

 so 

$$
A''(\tau,q)=
  \frac1\tau \max_i\frac{L_i}{nq_i}
  +
  \left(1-\frac1\tau\right)L.
$$

** Extreme case. ** When $\tau=1$, the constant is 

$$
A''(1,q)=\max_i\frac{L_i}{nq_i},
$$

 corresponding to non-uniform sampling SGD. Let $\tau\to\infty$, 

$$
A''(\tau,q)\to L,
$$

 correspond to full gradient descent. Since 

$$
\max_i\frac{L_i}{nq_i}\ge \frac1n\sum_i L_i\ge L,
$$

$A''(\tau,q)$ does not increase with $\tau$. 

** Optimal importance sampling. ** Minimize $\max_i L_i/(nq_i)$. The balance of the items at the optimal time: 

$$
q_i^*=\frac{L_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_j}.
$$

At this time 

$$
\max_i\frac{L_i}{nq_i^*}
  =
  \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_j.
$$

uniform sampling gives $\max_i L_i$, which may be significantly worse. 

** Optimal point prescription difference. ** Since $g(x^*)$ is the average of $\tau$ independent estimates, 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|\xi(x^*)\|^2
  =
  \operatorname{Var}(g(x^*))
  =
  \frac1\tau
  \operatorname{Var}\left(
    \frac{1}{nq_s}\nabla f_s(x^*)
  \right).
$$

 is explicitly written as 

$$
\operatorname{Var}(g(x^*))
  =
  \frac1\tau
  \left[
    \sum_{i=1}^{n}
    q_i
    \left\|
      \frac{1}{nq_i}\nabla f_i(x^*)-\nabla f(x^*)
    \right\|^2
  \right].
$$

**AC inequality and convergence. ** Obtain $C''(y)=0$ from expected smoothness, take $y=x^*$. The question is given for implication: 

$$
A=2A''(\tau,q),
  \qquad
  C=2\operatorname{Var}(g(x^*)).
$$

The acceptable step size range is 

$$
0<\gamma\le \frac{1}{A}=\frac{1}{2A''(\tau,q)}.
$$

Classic SGD The theorem gives 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|x_k-x^*\|^2
  \le
  (1-\gamma\mu)^k\|x_0-x^*\|^2
  +
  \frac{2\gamma}{\mu}\operatorname{Var}(g(x^*)).
$$

 which shows that minibatch reduces the noise floor by $1/\tau$ and importance sampling by reducing $A''$ relaxes the step size and improves the convergence constant.

**Question: D1: GloVe, Skip-gram, Node2Vec, TransE** Explain the data signal, target type and inductive bias respectively. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Fall, p. 8 **Official English prompt.** Embedding for Texts and Graphs. In 2--3 sentences each, describe the data signal, learning-objective type, and key inductive bias for GloVe, Skip-gram, Node2Vec, and TransE. **English interpretation.** Use 2--3 sentences for each of GloVe, Skip-gram, Node2Vec, and TransE: the data relationship signal used, the type of learning goal, and the core induction bias. 

**Answer. ** GloVe uses a global word-context co-occurrence matrix, targeting a weighted least squares fitted inner product with log-count, biased by assuming that word meaning can be expressed by a linear structure of global co-occurrence ratios. Skip-gram uses local window co-occurrence. The goal is to predict context words or use negative sampling for binary classification. The bias is that words in similar contexts have similar semantics. 

Node2Vec samples biased random walks on the graph, treats node sequences as sentences, and uses skip-gram to learn node embedding; the bias is controlled by BFS/DFS to balance homogeneity and structural equivalence. TransE uses the knowledge graph triple $(s,r,o)$, and the goal is to make $e_s+r$ close to $e_o$. The offset relationship can be approximated as embedding space translation.

**Question: D3: Simple MLN analysis** Two papers, predicate Cites and InTopic, two weighting formulas: citation propagation $w_1=1.5$, AI topic negative prior $w_2=-1.0$. Analyze ground network, probability, weight effects, and MAP. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Fall, p. 9 **Official English prompt.** A simple Markov Logic Network has formulas and weights for an academic-domain knowledge graph. **Solution.** Nodes are all ground atoms, such as $\operatorname{Cites}(P_i,P_j)$ and $\operatorname{InTopic}(P_i,\mathrm{AI})$. Each ground formula forms a clique: the citation propagation formula puts a citation atom and two topic atoms into the same clique; the a priori formula gives each topic atom an unary clique. 

Given world $x_1$: $P_1$ cites $P_2$, $P_1$ is AI, $P_2$ is not AI, and $P_2$ does not cite $P_1$. The formula 

$$
\operatorname{Cites}(p_1,p_2)\wedge \operatorname{InTopic}(p_1,\mathrm{AI})
  \Rightarrow
  \operatorname{InTopic}(p_2,\mathrm{AI})
$$

 is false under $(P_1,P_2)$ grounding; it is true under other groundings where the citation antecedent is false. If only two directed citation groundings are considered, then $n_1(x_1)=1$. The a priori formula $\operatorname{InTopic}(p,\mathrm{AI})$ is true for $P_1$ and false for $P_2$, so $n_2(x_1)=1$. The unnormalized probability is 

$$
\exp(1.5\cdot 1-1.0\cdot 1)=e^{0.5}.
$$

If $w_1$ is changed to $-1.5$, the model prefers "the paper cited by the AI paper is not AI" or at least does not satisfy the same topic communication; both papers are AI And the world where there are references will be significantly downgraded, because the originally satisfied propagation formula changes from reward to penalty. 

MAP: Given $\operatorname{Cites}(P_1,P_2)=\mathrm{true}$, if $P_1$ is an AI under the original weight, then $P_2$ should also be an AI to obtain the communication reward. But two AIs will pay two negative priors. Compare: 

$$
(0,0):0,\quad
  (1,0):-1,\quad
  (0,1):-1,\quad
  (1,1):1.5-2=-0.5.
$$

max is $(0,0)$. So MAP is AI for both. If the evidence strongly supports that $P_1$ is AI, then $P_2$ will be promoted as AI together.

**Question: D5: Text-to-image diffusion system** Write the closed distribution of forward process, ELBO, posterior mean, noise prediction training loss, and design text-conditioned Transformer denoiser. **Source location:** Official examination: 2025 Fall, p. 10--11 **Official English prompt.** Text-to-image diffusion system. **Answer. ** The diffusion ELBO, noise prediction target and text-conditioned denoiser design entries of this question are reviewed in [[02-primer#Diffusion and score|diffusion/score]] and [[02-primer#Transformer self-attention|Transformer self-attention]] respectively. 

$a$ Recursion 

$$
q(x_t|x_{t-1})=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\alpha_t}x_{t-1},\beta_t I),
  \qquad
  \bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_s.
$$

Induction can be obtained 

$$
q(x_t|x_0)
  =
  \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I).
$$

ELBO Writing 

$$
\log p_\theta(x_0)
  \ge
  \mathbb{E}_q\log p_\theta(x_0|x_1)
  -
  \sum_{t=2}^{T}
  \mathbb{E}_q\operatorname{KL}(q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)\|p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t))
  -
  \operatorname{KL}(q(x_T|x_0)\|p(x_T)).
$$

 the exact posterior is Gaussian: 

$$
q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)=\mathcal{N}(\tilde\mu_t(x_t,x_0),\tilde\beta_t I),
$$

$$
\tilde\beta_t=
  \frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\beta_t,
$$

$$
\tilde\mu_t
  =
  \frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\beta_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}x_0
  +
  \frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})}{1-\bar\alpha_t}x_t.
$$

$b$ If 

$$
x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\varepsilon,
$$

 then $x_0$ can be estimated by $x_t$ and prediction noise $\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t)$. Substituting into the posterior mean, we get 

$$
\mu_\theta(x_t,t)
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}
  \left(
    x_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t)
  \right).
$$

KL term is equivalent to the weighted noise MSE when the variance is fixed, so the training objective can be simplified to 

$$
\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\varepsilon}
  \left\|
    \varepsilon-
    \varepsilon_\theta(
      \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\varepsilon,
      t)
  \right\|^2.
$$

$c$ The Vincentian image system can use DiT-style denoiser: patchify the noisy image $x_t$ into tokens, add timestep embedding; text encoder generates token embeddings. Conditional injection methods include cross-attention, adaptive layer norm or splicing text tokens and image tokens. The reverse process is 

$$
p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t,T)
  =
  \mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta(x_t,t,\operatorname{Enc}(T)),\sigma_t^2I).
$$

 during training, randomly sampling $t$, adding noise images and prompts, optimizing conditional noise prediction; during inference, gradually denoising from $x_T\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, and using classifier-free guidance to improve text consistency.
