**Question: A2: The only global minimum point of a strongly convex function** If $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ is second-order continuously differentiable and $\lambda$-strongly convex, prove that its global minimizer is unique. 

**Source location:** Official examination: 2026 Spring, p. 2 **Official English prompt.** Let $f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$ be twice continuously differentiable and $\lambda$-strongly convex. **Answer. ** Strong convexity means that for $x\ne y$ and $t\in(0,1)$, 

$$
f(tx+(1-t)y)
  <
  tf(x)+(1-t)f(y).
$$

If there are two different global minimizers $x^*,y^*$, and the minimum value is $m$, then 

$$
f(tx^*+(1-t)y^*)
  <
  tm+(1-t)m=m,
$$

 and $m$ are global minimum conflicts. Therefore there is at most one minimizer. If the question implies that $f$ coercive or minimizer exists, then the minimizer is unique. For the second-order function of $\lambda$-strongly convex, the Hessian satisfies $\nabla^2f(x)\succeq \lambda I$, and usually also guarantees that $f(x)\to\infty$ when $\|x\|\to\infty$, so there is a global minimizer.

**Question: A4: Multi-class margin construction** sample $(x,y)$ is within its class center $\mu_y$ radius $r$ sphere, and the different centers are at least $4r$, $r\ge1$. Use the problem to multivector feature and 

$$
w_i=(\mu_i,-\|\mu_i\|^2/2)
$$

 to prove that there is $w$ so that the multiclass hinge loss of all samples is zero. **Source location:** Official examination: 2026 Spring, p. 3 **Official English prompt.** Consider examples $S\subset\mathbb R^n\times[k]$. There are centres $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_k\in\mathbb R^n$ such that $\|x-\mu_y\|\le r$ for each $(x,y)\in S$, where $r\ge 1$, and $\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|\ge 4r$ for $i\ne j$. Concatenate every instance with a bias coordinate $1$, and use the multivector construction $\Psi(x,y)\in\mathbb R^{k(n+1)}$ with $(x,1)$ in the $y$-th block. Show that there is $w\in\mathbb R^{k(n+1)}$ such that $\langle w,\Psi(x,y)\rangle>0$ for every $(x,y)\in S$. Hint: take blocks $w_i=[\mu_i,-\|\mu_i\|^2/2]$. **English interpretation.** Given $k$ class centers separated by at least $4r$, the distance of each sample to its true class center is no more than $r$. Put $(x,1)$ into the block vector $\Psi(x,y)$ of the corresponding category, proving that there is a multi-vector classifier $w$, so that all correctly labeled samples have positive intervals; the prompt is to take the $i$ block as $[\mu_i,-\|\mu_i\|^2/2]$. 

**Answer. ** For category $i$, the score is 

$$
s_i(x)=\langle \mu_i,x\rangle-\frac12\|\mu_i\|^2.
$$

If the true category of the sample is $y$, write 

$$
x=\mu_y+v,
  \qquad \|v\|\le r.
$$

 to compare the true category with any $j\ne y$: 

$$
\begin{aligned}
  s_y(x)-s_j(x)
  &=
  \langle \mu_y-\mu_j,x\rangle
  -\frac12(\|\mu_y\|^2-\|\mu_j\|^2)\\
  &=
  \frac12\|\mu_y-\mu_j\|^2
  +
  \langle \mu_y-\mu_j,v\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

 is separated by Cauchy’s inequality and center, 

$$
\langle \mu_y-\mu_j,v\rangle
  \ge
  -\|\mu_y-\mu_j\|\,\|v\|
  \ge
  -r\|\mu_y-\mu_j\|.
$$

 Let $d_{yj}=\|\mu_y-\mu_j\|\ge4r$, then 

$$
s_y(x)-s_j(x)
  \ge
  \frac12 d_{yj}^2-rd_{yj}
  =
  d_{yj}\left(\frac{d_{yj}}2-r\right)
  \ge
  4r(2r-r)=4r^2\ge 1.
$$

 Therefore for all $j\ne y$, the true class score is at least large $1$, multiclass hinge loss

$$
\max_{j\ne y}[1+s_j(x)-s_y(x)]_+
$$

 is $0$. 

**Question: A5: ReLU network and sigmoid network represent the same function** If the finite ReLU network and the finite sigmoid network represent the same scalar function on $\mathbb{R}^d$, prove that the function must be a constant. **Source location:** Official examination: 2026 Spring, p. 3 **Official English prompt.** Let $d\in\mathbb N$. Consider two feedforward neural networks with the same input dimension $d$ and scalar output. $\Phi_{\mathrm{ReLU}}$ uses ReLU in every hidden layer, while $\Phi_\sigma$ uses sigmoid. Suppose $\Phi_{\mathrm{ReLU}}(x)=\Phi_\sigma(x)$ for all $x\in\mathbb R^d$. Show that the represented function must be constant. **English interpretation.** Assume two feedforward networks with the same input dimension $d$ and scalar output, one uses ReLU for all hidden layers, and the other uses sigmoid; if the two represent the same function over the entire $\mathbb R^d$, it proves that the function can only be a constant. 

**Answer. ** The function represented by the ReLU network is piecewise affine. Sigmoid is real analytic, and the finite layer sigmoid network represents real analytic and bounded functions under a common feedforward structure without direct input skip. 

If the two are equal across $\mathbb{R}^d$, then the function is both real analytic and piecewise affine. If the pieced affine function has different affine pieces on both sides of a certain boundary hyperplane, its gradient will jump, making real analytic impossible. Therefore, the affine expressions of all pieces must be spelled into the same global affine function: 

$$
f(x)=a^\top x+b.
$$

On the other hand, the sigmoid network output is bounded, so the global affine function must satisfy $a=0$. Therefore $f(x)=b$ is a constant. 

**Exam Reminder. ** If the sigmoid network is allowed to have unrestricted linear skip output, the conclusion needs to additionally limit the network structure. Examination context usually defaults to hidden sigmoid feedforward network, and the output is a linear combination of bounded activations of the previous layer.

** Question: B1: backprop, sigmoid/ReLU, momentum, attention scaling, Transformer vs RNN** Answer five two-point questions. **Source location:** Official examination: 2026 Spring, p. 3--4 **Official English prompt.** (a) For an MLP $x_\ell=f_\ell(x_{\ell-1},\theta_\ell)$, $\ell=1,\ldots,L$, with loss $J=\mathcal L(x_L,y)$, derive $\partial J/\partial\theta_\ell$ by the chain rule and state why all gradients by backprop have the order of one forward evaluation. (b) Explain mathematically why $\sigma(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}$ causes vanishing gradients; contrast ReLU. (c) Write SGD-with-momentum updates for $v_{k+1}$ and $x_{k+1}$, and explain their benefit on oscillatory landscapes. (d) For $W=\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})$, explain $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ and what fails without it when $d_k$ is large. (e) Compare RNN and Transformer computational structure and explain why the latter is typically more efficient on modern hardware. **English interpretation.** This question is divided into five questions: derive the backpropagation of MLP and explain its complexity; explain the gradient disappearance of sigmoid and the mitigation mechanism of ReLU; write the momentum SGD update and explain its role in suppressing oscillation; explain the attention scaling factor $1/\sqrt{d_k}$; compare the computing structure and hardware efficiency of RNN and Transformer. 

**Answer. ** (a) For $x_\ell=f_\ell(x_{\ell-1},\theta_\ell)$, $J=\mathcal{L}(x_L,y)$, the chain rule gives 

$$
\frac{\partial J}{\partial \theta_\ell}
  =
  \frac{\partial J}{\partial x_L}
  \frac{\partial x_L}{\partial x_{L-1}}
  \cdots
  \frac{\partial x_{\ell+1}}{\partial x_\ell}
  \frac{\partial x_\ell}{\partial \theta_\ell}.
$$

[[02-primer#Backpropagation|Backpropagation]] reuses the intermediate adjoint $\delta_\ell=\partial J/\partial x_\ell$, and only does the local part once for each layer Jacobian-vector product, so the total complexity is of the same order as forward, differing by a constant factor. 

$b$ Sigmoid derivative 

$$
\sigma'(z)=\sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))\le 1/4.
$$

The deep chain product will decay exponentially; the derivative in the saturation region is closer to $0$. The ReLU derivative is $1$ at $z>0$, and the gradient on the activation path is not suppressed by the system, but at $z<0$ the derivative is $0$, which may be dead ReLU. 

$c$ Momentum SGD: 

$$
v_{k+1}=\beta v_k+\nabla f(x_k),
  \qquad
  x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta v_{k+1}.
$$

 random version using minibatch gradient. Momentum accumulates consistent directions, canceling out oscillations in high curvature directions, and is therefore faster in canyon-shaped loss landscapes. 

$d$ If the variance of each dimension of $q,k$ is about $1$, then the variance of $q^\top k$ is about $d_k$. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ stabilizes the logits variance. If scaling is removed, when $d_k$ is large, softmax will be saturated to nearly one-hot, the gradient will become smaller, and the training will be unstable. 

$e$ RNN must be recursive in time, and the dependency path of sequence length $L$ is long and difficult to parallelize. Each layer of Transformer [[02-primer#Transformer self-attention|self-attention]] can calculate all tokens in parallel, and one layer can interact between any tokens, which is more suitable for GPU/TPU batch matrix multiplication. 

**Question: B2: Flow matching and stochastic interpolant** From base distribution $\pi_0$ to data distribution $\pi_1$, use linear interpolation $x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1$ to define the velocity field and prove that the continuity equation, regression target and SDE with score have the same marginal. **Source location:** Official examination: 2026 Spring, p. 4--5 **Official English prompt.** Let $\pi_0$ be a simple base distribution on $\mathbb R^d$ and $\pi_1$ a data distribution. Seek $v_t$ such that $\frac d{dt}x_t=v_t(x_t)$, $x_0\sim\pi_0$, transports $\pi_0$ to $\pi_1$ at $t=1$. (a) For coupling $(x_0,x_1)\sim\gamma$ and $x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1$, compute $v_t(x_t\mid x_0,x_1)$. (b) Define $v_t(x)=\mathbb E[v_t(x_t\mid x_0,x_1)\mid x_t=x]$; show its flow satisfies $\partial_t\pi_t+\nabla\cdot(\pi_tv_t)=0$. (c) Give a squared regression objective for $v_\theta(t,x)$ and prove its minimizer is this marginal velocity. (d) Derive the Fokker--Planck equation of $dx_t=(v_t(x_t)+\beta_t\nabla_x\log\pi_t(x_t))dt+\sqrt{2\beta_t}\,dW_t$, show it has the same marginals, and explain score matching for the unknown score. **English interpretation.** Construct a flow matching vector field from the base distribution $\pi_0$ to the data distribution $\pi_1$. First, linear interpolation is used to obtain the conditional velocity, then the conditional expected velocity is defined and the continuity equation is proved; square regression is used to learn the parameterized velocity; finally, the Fokker--Planck equation of SDE with diffusion term is given, the conclusion of the same edge distribution is given, and how to use score matching to estimate the unknown score is given. 

**Answer. ** (a) The conditional velocity is 

$$
v_t(x_t|x_0,x_1)
  =
  \frac{d}{dt}x_t
  =
  x_1-x_0.
$$

$b$. Define marginal velocity

$$
v_t(x)=
  \mathbb{E}[x_1-x_0\mid x_t=x].
$$

 for any test function $\varphi$, 

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\varphi(x_t)
  =
  \mathbb{E}\langle \nabla\varphi(x_t),x_1-x_0\rangle
  =
  \mathbb{E}\langle \nabla\varphi(x_t),v_t(x_t)\rangle.
$$

If $\pi_t$ is $x_t$ density of 

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\int \varphi(x)\pi_t(x)\,dx
  =
  \int \langle \nabla\varphi(x),v_t(x)\rangle\pi_t(x)\,dx.
$$

integration by parts gives the weak form 

$$
\partial_t\pi_t+\nabla\cdot(\pi_t v_t)=0.
$$

 so ODE $dx_t/dt=v_t(x_t)$ yields the same set of marginals from $\pi_0$ transport to $\pi_1$. 

$c$ Use model $v_\theta(t,x)$ to minimize 

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta)
  =
  \mathbb{E}_{t,(x_0,x_1),x_t}
  \|v_\theta(t,x_t)-(x_1-x_0)\|^2.
$$

 versus fixed $(t,x)$, the optimal prediction of the squared loss is the conditional mean: 

$$
v_\theta^*(t,x)
  =
  \mathbb{E}[x_1-x_0\mid x_t=x]
  =
  v_t(x).
$$

$d$ Consider the SDE

$$
dx_t=
  \left(v_t(x_t)+\beta_t\nabla_x\log\pi_t(x_t)\right)dt
  +
  \sqrt{2\beta_t}\,dW_t.
$$

Fokker-Planck equation For 

$$
\partial_t p_t
  =
  -\nabla\cdot\left[
    \left(v_t+\beta_t\nabla\log\pi_t\right)p_t
  \right]
  +
  \beta_t\Delta p_t.
$$

if $p_t=\pi_t$, then 

$$
-\nabla\cdot(\beta_t\pi_t\nabla\log\pi_t)+\beta_t\Delta\pi_t
  =
  -\beta_t\nabla\cdot(\nabla\pi_t)+\beta_t\Delta\pi_t
  =
  0.
$$

 Then 

$$
\partial_t\pi_t=-\nabla\cdot(\pi_t v_t),
$$

 is consistent with the continuity equation of the deterministic ODE, so the marginals are the same. In practice, $\nabla\log\pi_t$ is unknown, and [[02-primer#Diffusion and score|denoising score matching]] can be used to train $s_\eta(t,x)\approx \nabla\log\pi_t(x)$.

**Answer. ** (a) [[02-primer#Bellman equation|Bellman equation]]：

$$

$$

  T^{\pi_k}V^{\pi_k}
  \quad\text{(policy evaluation)}

$$
\pi_{k+1}(s)\in
  \operatorname*{arg\,max}_a

  \right]
  \quad\text{(policy improvement)}.

$c$ Model-free actor-critic does not explicitly know $P$. critic uses sampling TD error

\max_\theta
  \mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim\pi_\theta}

    r_\psi(x,y)
    -
    \beta

  \right].
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\theta)
  =
  -
  \mathbb{E}
  \log
  \sigma
  \left[

    \left(
      \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^+|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^+|x)}

      \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^-|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^-|x)}

$$

**Answer. ** **C1-C5. ** [[02-primer#Proximal Operators and Composite Optimization|prox definition]] and [[02-primer#Proximal Optimality, Monotonicity, and Nonexpansiveness|prox nonexpansiveness derivation]] see chapter [[02-primer|Theoretical Foundations]]. The optimality of prox comes from the first-order condition of the convex problem: 

$$
0\in \partial R(x^+)+\frac1\gamma(x^+-u)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \frac{u-x^+}{\gamma}\in\partial R(x^+).
$$

Optimal point $x^*$ Satisfies 

$$
0\in \nabla f(x^*)+\partial R(x^*),
$$

 so 

$$
\frac{x^*-\gamma\nabla f(x^*)-x^*}{\gamma}
  =
  -\nabla f(x^*)\in\partial R(x^*),
$$

 is 

$$
x^*=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma R}(x^*-\gamma\nabla f(x^*)).
$$

if $r_x\in\partial R(x)$, $r_y\in\partial R(y)$, convex subgradient inequality gives 

$$
R(y)\ge R(x)+\langle r_x,y-x\rangle,
  \qquad
  R(x)\ge R(y)+\langle r_y,x-y\rangle.
$$

 added together to get 

$$
\langle r_x-r_y,x-y\rangle\ge0.
$$

 Let $x^+=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma R}(u)$, $y^+=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma R}(v)$. By optimality, 

$$
\frac{u-x^+}{\gamma}\in\partial R(x^+),
  \qquad
  \frac{v-y^+}{\gamma}\in\partial R(y^+).
$$

monotonicity gives 

$$
\langle u-v-(x^+-y^+),x^+-y^+\rangle\ge0.
$$

Therefore 

$$
\|x^+-y^+\|^2\le \langle u-v,x^+-y^+\rangle
  \le \|u-v\|\,\|x^+-y^+\|,
$$

i.e. [[02-primer#Proximal Optimality, Monotonicity, and Nonexpansiveness|prox nonexpansiveness]]. 

**C6-C8. ** Use fixed point and non-expansion: 

$$
\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_k-x^*-\gamma(g^k-\nabla f(x^*))\|^2.
$$

 conditional expectation, $g^k=\nabla f(x_k)+\xi^k$, $\mathbb{E}_k\xi^k=0$, get 

$$
\mathbb{E}_k\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_k-x^*-\gamma(\nabla f(x_k)-\nabla f(x^*))\|^2+\gamma^2\sigma^2.
$$

 $\gamma=2/(L+\mu)$ Using the strong convex-smooth joint inequality, we get 

$$
\mathbb{E}\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  (1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\|x_k-x^*\|^2+\gamma^2\sigma^2,
  \qquad
  \rho=\frac{4\mu L}{(L+\mu)^2}.
$$

The recursive limit noise floor is 

$$
\frac{\gamma^2\sigma^2}{\rho}.
$$

The meaning is: even if the mean direction shrinks, the random gradient injects variance at each step, and the error will not converge to zero under a constant step size. 

**C9. ** Order $\bar e_k=k^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k}e_t$. If $e_t$ are pairwise uncorrelated and $\mathbb{E}\|e_t\|^2\le V$, then 

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbb{E}\|\bar e_k\|^2
  &=
  \mathbb{E}\left\|
    \frac1k\sum_{t=1}^{k}e_t
  \right\|^2\\
  &=
  \frac1{k^2}\sum_{t=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\|e_t\|^2
  +
  \frac{2}{k^2}\sum_{s<t}\mathbb{E}\langle e_s,e_t\rangle\\
  &\le \frac{V}{k}.
\end{aligned}
$$

 This explains that the average iteration has less variance than the last iteration. 

**C10. ** When $R\equiv0$, by C7: 

$$
\mathbb{E}_t\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_t-x^*\|^2
  -2\gamma_t\langle\nabla f(x_t),x_t-x^*\rangle
  +\gamma_t^2\|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2
  +\gamma_t^2\sigma^2.
$$

convexity gives 

$$
\langle\nabla f(x_t),x_t-x^*\rangle\ge f(x_t)-f^*.
$$

 Smoothness gives 

$$
\|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2\le 2L(f(x_t)-f^*).
$$

 so 

$$
\mathbb{E}_t\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_t-x^*\|^2
  -
  2\gamma_t(1-L\gamma_t)(f(x_t)-f^*)
  +
  \gamma_t^2\sigma^2.
$$

if $\gamma_t\le 1/(2L)$, then 

$$
\mathbb{E}_t\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|^2
  \le
  \|x_t-x^*\|^2
  -
  \gamma_t(f(x_t)-f^*)
  +
  \gamma_t^2\sigma^2.
$$

 sums $t=1,\ldots,k$ and takes $\gamma_t=1/(\mu t)$, left side telescoping, the sum of the noise on the right side is 

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{k}\gamma_t^2\sigma^2=O(1).
$$

Using the weighted average in the strongly convex case or the question simplification prompt $\sum_{t=1}^{k}1/t\sim\log k$, we can get 

$$
\mathbb{E}[f(\bar x^k)-f^*]
  \le
  O\left(\frac{\log k}{k}\right).
$$
