## I. Preface

To further improve the caliber of doctoral students at Qiuzhen College, Tsinghua University, this syllabus is established for the PhD Qualifying Examination in Computational and Applied Mathematics. It applies to candidates taking Tsinghua University's doctoral entrance examination or qualifying examination.

## II. Examination Syllabus for Computational and Applied Mathematics

## Interpolation and Approximation:

·Trigonometric interpolation and approximation; fast Fourier transform; rational approximation; polynomial interpolation and spline interpolation; least-squares approximation.

## Numerical Solution of Nonlinear Equations:

·Convergence analysis of iterative methods for nonlinear equations and systems of nonlinear equations (bisection, Newton, quasi-Newton, and fixed-point iteration); polynomial root-finding algorithms.

## Numerical Linear Algebra:

·Direct and iterative algorithms for linear systems (classical and modern); numerical methods for eigenvalue problems; condition numbers; singular value decomposition; iterative algorithms for large sparse matrices.

## Numerical Solution of Ordinary Differential Equations:

·One-step and multistep methods; stability and convergence analysis; absolute stability; numerical methods for stiff problems.

## Numerical Solution of Partial Differential Equations:

·Finite-difference methods; finite-element methods; spectral methods; stability and convergence analysis; the Lax equivalence theorem.

## Mathematical Modeling, Numerical Simulation, and Applied Analysis:

·Dimensional analysis; asymptotic analysis and the method of stationary phase; boundary-layer analysis; quantitative analysis of mathematical models; Monte Carlo methods.

## Linear Programming and Optimization Methods:

·The simplex method; interior-point methods; penalty methods; Newton's method; homotopy methods; fixed-point iteration; dynamic programming.

## III. Assessment Objectives for Computational and Applied Mathematics

Candidates should demonstrate a thorough understanding and command of the examination content for Computational and Applied Mathematics, including fundamental definitions, properties, theorems and their proof ideas, internal connections, principal techniques, and basic applications of the principal theories. Candidates should be able to apply fundamental theories, methods, and techniques to prove or solve the corresponding problems.

## IV. Examination Format

Closed-book written examination; maximum score: 100 points.
