# Probability problems: team contest

## May 2019

1. Let Z be a real random variable, the log Laplace transform of Z is the function taking values in R ∪ $\{\infty\}$ defined for $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ by:

$$
\Psi_{Z}(\lambda) = \ln \operatorname{E}(\exp(\lambda Z)).
$$

1-1 For Z a random variable taking value in a bounded interval I, derive twice $\Psi_{Z}$ and identify, for all $\lambda,$ this second derivative to the variance of a random variable $Z_{\lambda}$ , which we precise its distribution.

1-2 If Z is a random variable taking value in a bounded interval I, show that the variance of $Z$ is upper bounded by $| I |^{2} / 4$

1-3 If Z is a random variable taking value in a bounded interval I. Show that for all $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$

$$
\Psi_{Z}(\lambda) \leq \frac{| I |^{2} \lambda^{2}}{8}.
$$

1-4 Let $Z_{1}, \ldots, Z_{n}$ be intendant random variables, such that $Z_{i}$ take value in $[a_{i}, b_{i}]$ ; denote $Z = \textstyle \sum Z_{i}$ and $\tilde{Z} = Z - \operatorname{E}(Z)$ . Show that

$$
\Psi_{\tilde{Z}}(\lambda) \leq \frac{\lambda^{2}}{8} \sum(b_{i} - a_{i})^{2}.
$$

1-5 With same notations as in 1-4, show that for all $\epsilon > 0$ 2

$$
\mathbb{P}(| \tilde{Z} | \geq \epsilon) \leq 2 \exp(- \frac{2 \epsilon^{2}}{\sum(b_{i} - a_{i})^{2}}).
$$

2. Let $\{X_{n}\}$ be independent indentically distributed random variables with finite mean, and $S_{n} =$ $\textstyle \sum_{i = 1}^{n} X_{i}$ be the partial sum. Show that the sequence $\{S_{n} / n\}$ is a reverse martingale. This means that

$$
\mathbb{E} \left[\frac{S_{n}}{n} \Big | \mathcal{F}_{n + 1} \right] = \frac{S_{n + 1}}{n + 1},
$$

where

$$
\mathcal{F}_{n} = \sigma \{S_{n}, S_{n + 1}, \dots\}
$$

is the smallest σ-algebra generated by the random variables $S_{k}$ for $k \geq n$

3. Let $n \geq 2$ be an integer, show that, a random vector $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ having independent components and its distribution is $O(n)$ invariant i.f.f. $X \sim{\mathcal{N}}(0, \sigma^{2})$

(Distribution is $O(n)$ invariant means that for any orthogonal matrix, that is a rotation, $P \in O(n)$ X and P X have the same distribution.)
