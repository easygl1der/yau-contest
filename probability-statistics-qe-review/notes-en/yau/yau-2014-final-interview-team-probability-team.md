1. Let X and Y be independent random variables, with Y uniformly distributed on $(0,1)$ and $P(X=0)=P(X=1/2)=1/2$. Define $W=Y/2+X$.

(1) Prove that W is also uniformly distributed on $(0,1)$.

(2) Determine $E[Y|W]$.

2. Let $X_{n}$ be an $n$-dimensional standard Gaussian random vector. Prove that the following probability converges to 1 as $n$ tends to infinity:

$$
P \big(\sqrt{n}(1 - \epsilon) \leq \| X_{n} \| \leq \sqrt{n}(1 + \epsilon) \big).
$$

3. Let $(X,Y)$ and $(Y',Z)$ be two discrete-valued random vectors, and Y and $Y'$ have the same distribution. Prove that there is a random vector $(\hat{X},\hat{Y},\hat{Z})$ such that $(\hat{X},\hat{Y})$ and $(\hat{Y},\hat{Z})$ have the same distribution as $(X,Y)$ and $(Y',Z)$ respectively.

4. Let $(\xi_{n})$ be independent random variables satisfying $P(\xi_n = 1) = p_n, P(\xi_n = 0) = 1 - p_n$. Assuming $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} p_kp_{k+1} < \infty$, prove that $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \xi_k \xi_{k+1}$ converges almost surely.
