Problem 1 (Probability) Let X be a real valued random variable such that

$$
\mathbb{E} f(X + 1) = \mathbb{E}(Xf(X))
$$

for all smooth function f with compact support.

(1) Show that X has a rapidly vanishing positive tail probability, i.e.,

$$
\mathbb{P} \{X > N\} \leq \frac{1}{N !}.
$$

(2) Use (1) or otherwise to show that X has the standard Poisson distribution $P(1)$

Problem 2 (Statistics) Let $X_{1}, \cdots, X_{m}$ be a random sample from a $N(\mu_{1}, \sigma_{1}^{2})$ distribution and let $Y_{1}, \cdots, Y_{n}$ be another random sample from $N(\mu_{2}, \sigma_{2}^{2})$ . Further, assume that

(a) $X_{1}, \cdots, X_{m}$ and $Y_{1}, \cdots, Y_{n}$ are independent;

(b) $m \geq 2$ and $n \geq 2 \mathrm{:}$

(c) $\mu_{1}$ and $\mu_{2}$ are real, and $\sigma_{1}$ and $\sigma_{2}$ are positive.

Finally, the parameters are all unknown.

(1) How to test $H_{0} : \sigma_{1}^{2} \le \sigma_{2}^{2}$ versus $H_{1} : \sigma_{1}^{2} > \sigma_{2}^{2} ?$

(2) If we cannot assume that the distributions for the $X_{i}^{\prime} s$ and $Y_{i}^{\mathrm{'}} \mathrm{{s}}$ are normal, how would you test whether the two samples come from the same distribution?
