6. (15 points) In the above definition of Galton-Watson branching process, Zn can be thought
  of as the number of descendants in the n-th generation, and X(j, n) can be thought of as the
  number of children of the j-th of these descendants. By tracking genealogical relationships, we
  obtain a tree T rooted at the single individual in generation 0 with a vertex for each individual
  in the progeny and an edge for each parent-child relationship. T is called a Galton-Watson
  tree.
  A property of rooted trees is said to be inherited if this property holds for all finite trees, and
  whenever it holds for a tree, it also holds for all subtrees rooted at the children of the root.
